FIRST VIRUS DECODE

RABIES VIRUS

Understanding one of the most lethal viral diseases known to science.

01 / OVERVIEW

What is Rabies?

Rabies is a viral zoonotic disease that affects the central nervous system of mammals, including humans. It is usually transmitted through the saliva of infected animals, especially through bites and scratches.


Once clinical symptoms appear, rabies is virtually 100% fatal. However, prompt preventive medical treatment after exposure can prevent the disease.

Scientific Identity

  • Virus: Rabies virus
  • Family: Rhabdoviridae
  • Genus: Lyssavirus
  • Type: RNA virus
  • Shape: Bullet-shaped
  • Main target: Nervous system
  • Transmission: Infected animal saliva
CHAPTER 01

The Origin of Rabies

A disease known to humanity for thousands of years.

Where did it originate?

The exact geographical origin of rabies is unknown. Historical records show that the disease has been recognized by human civilizations for more than 4,000 years.


Rabies is a zoonotic disease, meaning it can spread between animals and humans. It exists across most regions of the world, with a particularly serious impact in parts of Asia and Africa.


Ancient history

Historical records describe rabies-like diseases thousands of years ago.

1885 — Major scientific breakthrough

Louis Pasteur and his colleagues developed a successful rabies vaccine.

Modern era

Vaccination, improved surveillance and post-exposure treatment have made rabies preventable in humans.

CHAPTER 02

How Does It Affect Humans?

Understanding how rabies enters the body and affects the nervous system.

01. Transmission

The virus usually enters the human body through the bite or scratch of an infected mammal. Infected saliva can also enter through broken skin or the eyes and mouth.

02. Nervous System

The virus can travel through peripheral nerves towards the brain and spinal cord, where it causes severe inflammation.

03. Early Symptoms

Early symptoms may include fever, headache, weakness and unusual pain or tingling around the exposure site.

04. Advanced Disease

Advanced rabies can cause neurological problems, difficulty swallowing, abnormal behaviour and paralysis.

IMPORTANT MEDICAL FACT: Rabies is a medical emergency after a possible exposure. Never wait for symptoms before seeking medical attention.
CHAPTER 03

The Human Death Toll

The worldwide impact of rabies.

~59,000

Estimated annual deaths

The commonly cited WHO global estimate is approximately 59,000 human deaths annually. Actual estimates vary according to the methodology and year.

99%

Dog-mediated transmission

Approximately 99% of human rabies cases are linked to infected dogs.

40%

Children affected

Children under 15 account for approximately 40% of human rabies cases.

~100%

Fatality after symptoms

Once clinical symptoms develop, rabies is virtually always fatal.

Statistics are approximate global estimates, not a year-by-year historical death record.

CHAPTER 04

Is There a Cure?

The scientific reality

There is currently no reliably effective cure for rabies once clinical symptoms begin. Treatment at that stage is mainly supportive and focused on patient care.


However, rabies is preventable. Prompt post-exposure prophylaxis (PEP), provided before symptoms develop, can prevent the infection from progressing to disease.

Prevention before symptoms develop is the key. Do not delay medical assessment after an animal bite or possible exposure.
CHAPTER 05

Awareness & Prevention

What should a person do after a possible rabies exposure?

01

Wash immediately

Wash the affected area thoroughly with soap and running water for at least 15 minutes.

02

Seek medical care

Go to a hospital or medical facility urgently. Do not wait for symptoms.

03

Follow treatment

Follow the medical professional's advice about vaccination and other required preventive treatment.


How can we prevent rabies?

  • Vaccinate dogs and cats.
  • Avoid approaching unfamiliar animals.
  • Teach children safe behaviour around animals.
  • Report suspected rabid animals to the relevant authorities.
  • Seek medical attention after possible exposure.
CHAPTER 06

Available Medication

Modern medical science has developed effective ways to prevent rabies.

Rabies Vaccine

The human rabies vaccine helps the immune system develop protection against the virus. It is used before or after a potential exposure, according to medical advice.

Rabies Immunoglobulin (RIG)

Rabies immunoglobulin provides ready-made antibodies and may be recommended for certain serious exposures in people who have not previously been vaccinated.

Monoclonal Antibodies

Certain approved monoclonal antibody products can be used as an alternative to rabies immunoglobulin in some medical protocols.

Pre-exposure Vaccination

Vaccination before exposure may be recommended for certain people at increased risk of encountering the virus.

These treatments are preventive medical interventions, not a home remedy or a cure for symptomatic rabies. A qualified healthcare professional must determine the appropriate treatment.
